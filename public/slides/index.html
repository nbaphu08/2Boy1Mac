02 — The problemSpaceMuse · Team 2Boy1Mac

They are still out there.
Few people know
their stories.

Since 1967, NASA has landed machines on the Moon and Mars. When their work was done, none of them came home. NASA's own catalogue says the first step in preserving them is establishing exactly what is there and where it is.

Invisible

Unknown

Hundreds of human-made objects rest on the Moon alone. Most students cannot name one.

Abstract

Flat

They are taught as names and dates — not as places with a reason, a result and a present.

At risk

Unprotected

As new landers head back to the Moon, these first sites beyond Earth need people who care.

03 — Our solutionSpaceMuse · Team 2Boy1Mac

An expedition,
not a list

One continuous, scroll-driven 3D journey. The visitor is given a mission — find the machines we left behind — and earns each discovery by flying there.

01 · Launch

SLS / Orion from Launch Complex 39B at dusk.

02 · Lunar orbit

Five glowing targets at their real coordinates.

03 · Land & track

Touch down, then fly low from site to site.

04 · Mars entry

Seven more targets: entry, descent, landing.

05 · Mars search

Low flights between seven sites, to Ingenuity.

06 · The answer

Hall of Silence, then: junk or history?

Place-based

Go there

Real landing coordinates, site cues (Hadley Rille, Troy, Twin Peaks) and real NASA maps.

Honest

Real facts, imagined trip

For newcomers

No prior knowledge

A mission briefing, plain-language labels and a voice guide — built for a student's first visit.

05 — The collectionSpaceMuse · Team 2Boy1Mac

12 machines,
12 lessons

Each exhibit ends with what it taught us — so a discarded machine becomes a discovery, not just debris.

Moon · 1969 Still active

Apollo 11

Its mirrors show the Moon drifts about 3.8 cm farther from Earth every year.

Moon · 1967

Surveyor 3

Its camera, brought home by Apollo 12, showed how hardware survives on the Moon.

Moon · 1971

Apollo 15 Rover

The first car on another world; this crew found the Genesis Rock, original lunar crust.

Moon · 1972

Apollo 17

Orange soil here: glass beads from volcanoes that erupted billions of years ago.

Moon · 2012

GRAIL Ebb & Flow

Mapped lunar gravity: the crust is only about 34–43 km thick.

Mars · 1976

Viking 1

Carried the first experiments to search for life on Mars.

Mars · 1997

Pathfinder

Proved the airbag landing that Spirit and Opportunity later used.

Mars · 2004

Spirit

Silica-rich soil: hot water once soaked this ground.

Mars · 2004

Opportunity

"Blueberries" and mineral veins: evidence of past liquid water.

Mars · 2008

Phoenix

Confirmed water ice just below the surface and found perchlorate.

Mars · 2018

InSight

Marsquakes revealed the planet's layers, including a liquid metal core.

Mars · 2021

Ingenuity

First powered flight on another planet, then a scout for Perseverance.

07 — The message & what's nextSpaceMuse · Team 2Boy1Mac
"Are these machines junk, or history?"
They are not junk.
They are history.
2011

NASA asks future missions to keep away from Apollo sites — down to footprints and rover tracks.

2020

U.S. law: the One Small Step to Protect Human Heritage in Space Act.

Impact

A free, one-click classroom visit to the first heritage sites beyond Earth.

Next

More sites (Luna, Chang'e, Apollo 12 / 14 / 16) and teacher worksheets.

Reach

More languages, starting with Vietnamese; updates as Artemis returns.
