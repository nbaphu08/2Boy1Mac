The problem

Hundreds of objects are still out there.
Almost nobody knows their stories.

  • Since 1967, NASA has landed machines on the Moon and Mars. None of them came home.
  • NASA's own catalogue says the first step in preserving them is knowing what is there and where it is.
  • For students they are names in a list — not places, not stories, not heritage.
  • As the Moon gets busy again, these sites need people who know why they matter.

04 / 07
Built on NASA open data

Every number has a source

  • NSSDCA — dates, dimensions, landing coordinates
  • LRO / LROC — "now" images, GRAIL crater positions
  • HiRISE / MRO — Mars landers seen from orbit
  • NASA SVS 4014 — GRAIL gravity map in the simulation
  • NASA 3D Resources — LM, Viking, MER, InSight, Ingenuity
  • NASA History Office — lunar artifact catalogue (2012)
  • Image & Video Library — Apollo / mission photos
  • CGI Moon Kit · Viking MDIM — planet textures

Real NASA photos are shown unmodified with credits. 3D scenes are illustrative reconstructions built in code from NASA dimensions and imagery.

The message

"Are these machines junk, or history?"

They are not junk.
They are history.

  • 2011 — NASA asks future missions to keep away from Apollo sites, down to footprints and rover tracks.
  • 2020 — U.S. law: the One Small Step to Protect Human Heritage in Space Act.
  • The first historic sites beyond Earth — and each one taught us something.
