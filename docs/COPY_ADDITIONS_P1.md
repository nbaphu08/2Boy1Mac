## 3b. How it worked, plot twist, NASA's Eyes (Opus, 15 Nov 2026)

> Shown in each exhibit panel under "HOW DID IT WORK?" (Built to → How it worked → What we learned). One source per step; mechanism wording re-checked against the source pages on 15 Nov 2026. Panel labels: `HOW DID IT WORK?`, `PLOT TWIST`, `EXPLORE IT YOURSELF`, and the note "NASA's Eyes on the Solar System is a free 3D app built on real NASA mission data. Opens in a new tab."

**SM-L-001 · Apollo 11**
- *Built to:* Eagle's lower half was a landing stage and a launch pad in one. It set the crew down, then stayed behind so the upper half could carry them back to orbit. [S5]
- *How it worked:* The reflector is a panel of 100 small corner-cube prisms with no power at all. Observatories on Earth aim a laser at it and clock how long the light takes to come back. [S7]
- *What we learned:* Fifty years of these measurements show that the Earth and Moon are slowly drifting apart: about 3.8 cm a year, roughly as fast as your fingernails grow. [S7]

**SM-L-002 · Surveyor 3**
- *Built to:* Prove that a spacecraft could land softly on the Moon, and find out how firm the ground was before astronauts tried to walk on it. [S8]
- *How it worked:* Radar timed the firing of a solid-fuel braking rocket. Then three small throttled engines, guided by radar, lowered it to the surface. A scoop on a 1.5 m arm dug the soil while a TV camera watched. [S8]
- *What we learned:* Apollo 12 landed 155 m away and brought its camera and scoop home, so scientists could study what two and a half years on the Moon does to a spacecraft. [S9]
- **Plot twist — Germs on the Moon?** Scientists found living Streptococcus mitis bacteria in a piece of foam inside the returned camera. Had Earth germs survived two and a half years on the Moon? Probably not: no other camera part held bacteria, and researchers later suggested the foam was contaminated back on Earth, during analysis in the Lunar Receiving Laboratory. [S71]

**SM-L-003 · Apollo 15 Lunar Roving Vehicle**
- *Built to:* Carry two astronauts far beyond the distance they could safely walk in their spacesuits. [S12]
- *How it worked:* It flew to the Moon folded up on the side of the lunar module. Two batteries powered a separate electric motor in each of its four wheels, driven with a T-shaped hand controller. [S10]
- *What we learned:* Over three moonwalks it carried the crew 27.9 km, to the foot of the Apennine Mountains and the edge of Hadley Rille. [S12]

**SM-L-004 · Apollo 17**
- *Built to:* Survey and sample the Taurus–Littrow valley. Its crew included Harrison Schmitt, a geologist and the first scientist-astronaut to land on the Moon. [S18]
- *How it worked:* The crew spent about 75 hours on the surface, set up an automated science station and drove a battery-powered rover from stop to stop. [S18]
- *What we learned:* They brought home 110.5 kg of rock and soil, including soil made almost entirely of tiny beads of orange glass. [S19]

**SM-L-005 · GRAIL — Ebb & Flow**
- *Built to:* Map the Moon's gravity in detail, to learn what lies hidden beneath its surface. [S72]
- *How it worked:* Twin spacecraft, Ebb and Flow, flew one behind the other. Over areas of stronger gravity they moved slightly toward or away from each other, and an instrument measured every change. [S72]
- *What we learned:* The highest-resolution gravity map of any world, showing a crust only 34 to 43 km thick on average. [S24]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Ebb and Flow in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_grail_a

**SM-M-001 · Viking 1 Lander**
- *Built to:* Land safely on Mars, photograph it, and run experiments that looked for signs of life in its soil. [S27]
- *How it worked:* Two nuclear power generators kept it running. A sampler arm scooped soil into a small onboard laboratory, while cameras and a weather boom watched the world around it. [S27]
- *What we learned:* Its biology experiments found unexpected, puzzling chemistry in the soil, but no clear evidence of living microbes. [S27]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Viking 1 in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_viking_1_lander

**SM-M-002 · Mars Pathfinder & Sojourner**
- *Built to:* Test a new way of landing on Mars and carry a small rover, Sojourner, to explore around the lander. [S28]
- *How it worked:* A parachute slowed it down. About 100 m above the ground, giant airbags inflated, and the lander bounced across the surface like a beach ball before rolling to a stop. [S28]
- *What we learned:* Sojourner rolled down a ramp and measured what rocks and soils were made of. Together, lander and rover found that Mars may once have been warm and wet. [S28]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Mars Pathfinder in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_mars_pathfinder

**SM-M-003 · Spirit**
- *Built to:* Work as a robot geologist, looking for signs of past water on Mars. [S32]
- *How it worked:* When one front wheel stopped turning, Spirit kept driving and dragged it along. The stuck wheel scraped away the top layer of soil as the rover moved. [S33]
- *What we learned:* Underneath lay bright, nearly pure silica, probably made by hot springs or steam vents. Strong evidence that ancient Mars was much wetter. [S33]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Spirit in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_mars_exploration_rover_2

**SM-M-004 · Opportunity**
- *Built to:* Like its twin Spirit, work as a robot geologist looking for signs of past water on Mars. [S32]
- *How it worked:* Its toolkit included a Rock Abrasion Tool to grind into rocks, a microscopic imager and two spectrometers to tell what the rocks were made of. [S47]
- *What we learned:* The rocks told a water story: hematite "blueberries" formed by acidic groundwater, and later, clays formed in water friendly enough for ancient microbes. [S49]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Opportunity in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_mars_exploration_rover_1

**SM-M-005 · Phoenix**
- *Built to:* Land in the far north of Mars and dig for water ice. [S53]
- *How it worked:* A 2.5 m robotic arm dug trenches and delivered soil to tiny ovens on the deck, which heated the samples to see what gases came out. [S53]
- *What we learned:* Bright clumps in a trench vanished within days, and the ovens confirmed water in a soil sample. The team also found perchlorate salts in the soil. [S53]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Phoenix in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_phoenix

**SM-M-006 · InSight**
- *Built to:* Study the deep inside of Mars, not its surface. [S56]
- *How it worked:* Its 1.8 m robotic arm lifted a seismometer from the deck and placed it on the ground, where it listened for marsquakes. [S56]
- *What we learned:* Reading the waves from 1,319 marsquakes, scientists mapped the layers of Mars, down to a liquid core about 1,800 km in radius. [S58]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See InSight in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_insight

**SM-M-007 · Ingenuity**
- *Built to:* Find out whether a helicopter could fly in the air of Mars, which is less than 1% as dense as Earth's. [S61]
- *How it worked:* A solar panel charged its batteries, with enough energy for one flight of about 90 seconds per Martian day. Its rotor blades spanned about 1.2 m, tip to tip. [S61]
- *What we learned:* Planned for up to five flights, it flew 72, scouting ahead for the Perseverance rover. [S62]
- NASA's Eyes link: "See Perseverance, which carried Ingenuity, in NASA's Eyes" → https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/#/sc_mars_2020
