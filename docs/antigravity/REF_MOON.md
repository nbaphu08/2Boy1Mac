# REF_MOON — Visual & dimensional reference for Hall I (for Antigravity)

Use these real dimensions and features so every model and diorama is faithful. Reference photos are in `public/media/thennow/` (`*-1600.jpg`) and `materials/thennow/` (originals). If a detail is not listed here, match the reference photo — do **not** invent markings, text or logos.
Scale convention suggestion: **1 scene unit = 1 meter** inside each exhibit diorama.

---

## SM-L-001 · Apollo 11 — Eagle descent stage + Laser Ranging Retroreflector (Sea of Tranquility)
**Source:** NSSDCA 1969-059C; NASA Catalogue of Manmade Material on the Moon.
- Descent stage: **octagonal prism 4.2 m across, 1.7 m thick**; bottom of stage **1.5 m above the ground**; **4 legs** with **round footpads**; **9.4 m** between opposite footpads; **~1 m conical descent-engine skirt** under the stage.
- One leg carries a **small egress platform and ladder**; the **commemorative plaque is mounted on a strut behind the ladder** (show as a small plate, no readable text required).
- Ascent stage **left** (launched back to orbit) → show the **top deck empty** (clip the existing LM model; see `ExhibitStage3D.tsx`).
- Surface finish (from photos `as11-40-5948`): gold/amber and black thermal foil around the stage, silver/black on legs.
- **EASEP** package ~**17 m south** of the LM: square base, two canted rectangular solar panels facing east and west, a short cylindrical antenna mast (mass 48 kg).
- **LRRR** deployed ~**5 m NNW of the EASEP**: a flat, tilted panel holding **100 corner-cube prisms in a 10 × 10 grid**, on a small stand; **no power, no cables**. It is the only exhibit `STILL ACTIVE` → a faint, thin **green laser** line coming down from the sky is appropriate (lunar laser ranging uses green 532 nm lasers).
- Ground: footprints around the LM, the US flag was knocked over at liftoff (do NOT add a flag unless lying flat; simplest: omit).
- Photos: `SM-L-001_then_as11-40-5948` (Aldrin, EASEP, LM), `SM-L-001_now_PIA12909` (LRO from orbit).

## SM-L-002 · Surveyor 3 (Ocean of Storms) — needs a NEW model (AG-3a)
**Source:** NSSDCA 1967-035A.
- **About 3 m tall.** Structure: **tripod of thin-walled aluminum tubing** with braces.
- **Central mast** extends ~**1 m above the tripod apex**; on top: a **flat solar panel (0.855 m², 792 cells)** on a positioner and a **flat planar high-gain antenna**.
- **3 hinged landing legs** at the lower corners, each with shock absorber + crushable honeycomb blocks, ending in **footpads ~4.3 m from the center** (footpad radius from center ≈ 4.3 m → leg span ≈ 8.6 m tip-to-tip).
- **2 omnidirectional conical antennas** on the ends of **folding booms**.
- **TV survey camera** near the top of the tripod with an extended **glare hood**; two small flat mirrors on the frame.
- **Surface sampler:** scoop **12 × 5 cm** on a **1.5 m pantograph arm**.
- Finish: **white paint**, polished aluminum underside; spherical tanks (vernier fuel) and a spherical retrorocket case (the retrorocket was jettisoned before landing — **do not** model it attached).
- **Current state:** Apollo 12 astronauts removed the **camera and the scoop** in Nov 1969 → show the camera mount and sampler arm **without** the camera head/scoop; dust-tan discoloration.
- **Context:** sits on the inner slope of a small crater; the Apollo 12 LM (Intrepid) landed **155 m away** — a distant LM silhouette on the crater rim is a strong storytelling detail.
- Photos: `SM-L-002_then_AS12-48-7134` (Bean + Surveyor + LM behind), `SM-L-002_then-alt_as12-48-7121` (Surveyor in its crater).

## SM-L-003 · Apollo 15 Lunar Roving Vehicle (Hadley–Apennine) — needs a NEW model (AG-3a)
**Source:** NSSDCA "The Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle".
- **Frame length 3.1 m; wheelbase 2.3 m; max height 1.14 m; ground clearance 36 cm.** Mass 210 kg.
- **4 wire-mesh wheels**: tire **81.8 cm diameter, 23 cm wide**, woven zinc-coated steel strands on aluminum rim/discs; **titanium chevrons** on ~50% of the contact area; inner bump-stop frame 64.8 cm.
- **Dust guards (fenders)** above each wheel.
- **Two side-by-side folding tubular aluminum seats** with nylon webbing, footrests; **T-shaped hand controller** between the seats.
- **Mesh dish high-gain antenna on a mast at the front center**; **TV camera** at the front.
- Frame: welded aluminum tubing, aluminum floor panels; three-part hinged chassis.
- **Parking:** left ~**150 m (500 ft) east of the LM**, camera aimed at the LM to televise the liftoff.
- Environment: mare plain with **Mount Hadley** and the Apennine front in the background; **Hadley Rille** nearby.
- Photos: `SM-L-003_then_as15-88-11901` (LRV against mountains), `SM-L-003_now_GSFC_20171208_Archive_e001054` (LRO view of Hadley Rille & Mt Hadley).
- Optional micro-details for the vitrine (small, no text): Fallen Astronaut figurine + plaque lying on the ground near the rover is NOT at the rover position — keep it out of the diorama.

## SM-L-004 · Apollo 17 — Challenger descent stage, rover, footprints (Taurus–Littrow)
- Reuse the LM descent stage (same as SM-L-001) + reuse the LRV model (AG-3a) parked a short distance away.
- **Key storytelling feature:** **rover tracks and footprints** clearly visible on the ground (they are visible from orbit in LRO images).
- Environment: a **valley ringed by mountains** (massifs on both sides).
- Photos: `SM-L-004_then_as17-147-22526` (Cernan driving), `SM-L-004_now_LRO-apollo17-litho`.

## SM-L-005 · GRAIL Ebb & Flow (impact site near the north pole) — needs a NEW model (AG-3a)
**Source:** GRAIL launch press kit (JPL, Aug 2011); NASA Science GRAIL pages.
- Each spacecraft: main structure **1.09 m high × 0.95 m wide × 0.76 m deep**, roughly **washing-machine sized**, ~**200 kg**; **solar arrays: four panels** of silicon cells (exact panel layout not confirmed — use the artist concept `PIA13965` for the look: box bus with two solar wings).
- Twins **Ebb (GRAIL-A)** and **Flow (GRAIL-B)** flew one behind the other.
- **The exhibit is the impact site, not intact spacecraft:** both hit the **southern slope of a ~2.5 km mountain** near Goldschmidt crater on 17 Dec 2012, forming **two craters ~5 m wide**, about **2.2 km apart**, with **faint dark ejecta**.
- Suggested diorama: a mountain slope with two small fresh craters (dark ejecta), plus a **translucent "ghost" hologram** of the two spacecraft above the craters to show what they were. Label it in the UI as *Artist's reconstruction* (Sonnet handles labels).
- Photos: `SM-L-005_then_PIA13965` (artist concept), `SM-L-005_now_GRAIL_impacts_before_after` (LROC before/after).

## Global look for Hall I
- Regolith: medium-dark grey with slightly brown tint (match `moon_color_4k.jpg`), fine grain, small craters, scattered rocks; harsh single-direction sunlight + **cold museum spotlight `#DCE6FF`** from above; deep black sky with stars; Earth may hang in the sky only for near-side sites (Apollo 11, 12, 15, 17), **not** for the GRAIL polar site (Earth low on horizon at best).
- Dust motes: very sparse, slow; no atmosphere haze on the Moon.
- No invented text, flags, logos or numbers on any model.
