# REF_MARS — Visual & dimensional reference for Hall II (for Antigravity)

Same rules as REF_MOON: real dimensions, match the reference photos (`public/media/thennow/SM-M-*`), **no invented text/logos/numbers**. Scale: 1 unit = 1 m.

## Global look for Hall II
- Ground: rusty orange-brown regolith, scattered dark basalt rocks, fine dust ripples; match `mars_color_4k.jpg` tones.
- Sky: daytime **butterscotch / tan haze** near the horizon fading upward; thin atmosphere → soft shadows, light dust haze (fog) — unlike the Moon.
- Dust coverage is part of the story: most machines are **dust-coated** (orange tint on white/silver surfaces, dimmed solar panels).
- Keep the cold museum spotlight `#DCE6FF` as the shared museum language, but warm the ambient light to the Martian sky.

## SM-M-001 · Viking 1 Lander (Chryse Planitia) — model exists (`SM-M-001_viking_lander.web.glb`)
**Source:** NSSDCA 1975-075C.
- **Six-sided aluminum base** with alternating **1.09 m and 0.56 m** sides; instruments on top.
- **3 legs** on the short sides; footpads form an **equilateral triangle with 2.21 m sides** (top view).
- **2 RTGs** on opposite sides under wind screens (each 28 cm tall, 58 cm diameter).
- **Steerable parabolic high-gain dish** on a boom near one edge; **sampler arm** from one long side (collector head); **2 cylindrical scan cameras** near one long side; **meteorology boom** rising from the top of one leg.
- Mass ~**572–600 kg** (sources vary — don't display).
- Scene: rock-strewn plain (see first surface image `PIA00381` with the footpad).

## SM-M-002 · Mars Pathfinder lander + Sojourner (Ares Vallis) — needs NEW models (AG-3b)
**Sources:** Mars Pathfinder landing press kit; JPL release; NSSDCA MESURPR.
- **Lander:** a **tetrahedron** about **0.9–1 m tall** when closed; **3 triangular petals + base petal**; opened it spans **2.75 m across**; **camera on a mast ~1.5 m above the ground**; solar cells on the petal tops; **deflated airbags** bunched around the petals; **ramps** unrolled from a petal to the ground.
- **Sojourner:** **65 cm long × 48 cm wide × 30 cm tall** (NSSDC: 63 × 48 × 28 cm), **10.6 kg**, **6 wheels** on a rocker-bogie, flat **solar panel on top**, small whip antenna. In the diorama Sojourner sits **near a rock a few meters from the lander** (the "Yogi" rock in photos).
- Photos: `SM-M-002_then_PIA00614` (airbags + rover on petal), `SM-M-002_then-alt_PIA00632` (rover + terrain).

## SM-M-003 · Spirit (Gusev → "Troy") and SM-M-004 · Opportunity (Meridiani → Endeavour) — model exists (`SM-M-003-004_mer_spirit_opportunity.web.glb`)
**Source:** NASA MER landings press kit.
- Rover **1.5 m high × 2.3 m wide × 1.6 m long**, **174 kg**; 6 wheels rocker-bogie; flat solar wings; tall camera mast (Pancam); robotic arm.
- **Spirit state:** wheels **sunk up to the hubs in soft soil** at Troy, beside the low plateau **Home Plate**; bright disturbed soil; dusty panels. Drove the last part with **only 5 working wheels** (one wheel visibly dragging-gouged soil is a strong detail).
- **Opportunity state:** heavy **dust coating** after the 2018 planet-wide storm; hazy reddish sky darker than other exhibits (storm mood, but keep visibility); on a slope of **Perseverance Valley at the rim of Endeavour Crater**.
- Photos: `SM-M-003_then_PIA05040`, `SM-M-003_now_PIA12205`; `SM-M-004_then_PIA17759` (self-portrait), `SM-M-004_now_PIA22549`, `SM-M-004_then-lastmessage_PIA22929`.

## SM-M-005 · Phoenix (Vastitas Borealis, arctic) — needs NEW model (AG-3b)
**Source:** Phoenix landing press kit (JPL, May 2008).
- **Deck diameter 1.5 m**; **two round/decagonal solar arrays**, **span 5.52 m deployed**; **robotic arm 2.35 m** with a scoop; mast camera (SSI) and a small **meteorology mast** (with lidar pointing up); 3 legs.
- **Current state (tell this story):** solar panels **bent/broken**, very likely by the weight of **CO₂ ice** in winter (HiRISE images showed damage) → model one array cracked/collapsed.
- Ground: **polygon-patterned arctic terrain** (shallow troughs forming polygons), a few **trenches dug by the arm**, bright patches of exposed ice in a trench.
- Photos: `SM-M-005_then_PIA11719` (self-portrait), `SM-M-005_now_PIA22223`.

## SM-M-006 · InSight (Elysium Planitia) — model exists (`SM-M-006_insight_panels_deployed.web.glb`)
**Source:** NASA Science InSight spacecraft page.
- **6 m long with solar panels deployed**; **deck diameter 1.56 m**; two round solar arrays; robotic arm (~1.8 m) on the deck.
- Instruments on the ground in front of the lander: the **seismometer under a dome-shaped wind and thermal shield**, and the **heat-flow probe ("the mole") housing** next to it.
- **Current state:** solar panels **heavily dust-covered** (compare first vs final selfie) — the reason the mission ended.
- Ground: flat, smooth, few rocks ("a plain chosen for safety").
- Photos: `SM-M-006_then_PIA22876` (first selfie, clean), `SM-M-006_then-final_PIA25287` (final, dusty), `SM-M-006_now_PIA23376`.

## SM-M-007 · Ingenuity ("Valinor Hills", Jezero Crater) — model exists (`SM-M-007_ingenuity.web.glb`)
**Source:** Ingenuity landing press kit; NASA Science resources.
- **0.49 m tall**, **1.8 kg**; **two coaxial counter-rotating rotors ~1.2 m (1.21 m) diameter**, 4 carbon-fiber blades; **fuselage 13.6 × 19.5 × 16.3 cm**; **4 landing legs ~0.38 m**; small solar panel on top of the rotor mast.
- **Current state:** final hard landing on **flight 72** damaged the **rotor blades** → show one blade broken/missing a tip. Sits among sand ripples/dunes of "Valinor Hills".
- **Debris vitrine nearby (optional, small):** Perseverance's **parachute and cone-shaped backshell** photographed by Ingenuity on flight 26 (`SM-M-007_vitrine-backshell_PIA25219`). Could be a distant element on the landscape — do not place it at Valinor Hills itself (different site); a separate mini-plinth is fine.
- Photos: `SM-M-007_then-alt_PIA26236`, `SM-M-007_then_PIA26237`, `SM-M-007_now_PIA25174`.
