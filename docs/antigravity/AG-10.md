## Part 1 — GRAIL: a short simulation of what Ebb & Flow did (beat 12, `moon-grail`)
The humans asked: "the two GRAIL spacecraft Ebb & Flow have no simulation". Today the beat only shows two craters and faint holograms. Make it tell the mission in ~14 s.

**Verified facts to depict** (from `docs/EXHIBITS.md` SM-L-005 and `REF_MOON.md`):
- Twins **Ebb (GRAIL-A)** and **Flow (GRAIL-B)**, washing-machine-sized box bus with solar wings (look: `public/media/thennow/SM-L-005_then_PIA13965-1600.jpg`, artist concept), flew **one behind the other in the same lunar orbit** and mapped the Moon's gravity by precisely measuring the changing distance between them.
- Mission end: **17 Dec 2012**, both deliberately flew into the **southern slope of a ~2.5 km mountain near Goldschmidt crater, near the north pole** — Ebb first, Flow shortly after — leaving **two craters ~5 m wide** with faint dark ejecta (Sally K. Ride Impact Site).
- The real result: the GRAIL **free-air gravity map** (download it yourself — NASA SVS 4014, public domain, credit "NASA/ARC-MIT/JPL-Caltech/GSFC"): `https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004000/a004014/LDAM_GRGM1200A_007_700_16PPD.0centered.jpg` (5760×2880 equirectangular, 0° longitude at centre; red = stronger gravity / mascons, blue = weaker). Save resized copies to `public/media/three/grail/` (2K WebP for medium/low, 4K for high).

**Sequence** (time-based, plays automatically once when the visitor settles on the beat — |p − 12| < 0.05 for 0.4 s — and again each time they come back; scrolling away interrupts it cleanly):
1. **Mapping (0–6 s):** camera in lunar orbit; the Moon globe below (existing LROC texture). Ebb leads, Flow follows on the same polar orbit, joined by a thin pulsing starlight `#9DB8FF` link (the ranging signal). As they pass, the gravity map **paints onto the globe swath by swath** under their ground track (Moon turning beneath the polar orbit, time-compressed), until the whole globe is revealed; mascons glow softly.
2. **Descent (6–11 s):** map fades back to grey; camera follows the pair down toward the north-polar mountain on a low, grazing approach.
3. **Impacts (11–13 s):** Ebb hits first, then Flow a moment later beside it: short flash + **ballistic ejecta** (no air on the Moon → dust flies in clean arcs and falls straight back, no billowing cloud).
4. **Rest (13 s →):** ends on the current rest composition: the mountain slope with **two small fresh craters with dark ejecta**; drop the old floating holograms (or keep them as a very faint ghost — your call).
- **Replay hook:** export from a tiny module, e.g. `components/journey/three/simulationBus.ts`, a `replayGrailSim()` function (and subscription) so Sonnet can wire a "Replay simulation" button in the DOM card. Say the exact API in the handoff.
- Reduced motion: no autoplay; show the final rest frame (and optionally the full gravity-map globe small in the sky? no — keep it simple: rest frame).
- Perf: low tier may skip the swath painting (show the map fading in at once) and use fewer ejecta particles.

## Part 2 — Mars Pathfinder + Sojourner rebuild (beat 15, `mars-pathfinder`)
The humans find it "ugly and not real". Problems in `docs/antigravity/shots/review-AG9-before.jpg` (row 3, left): airbags look like puffy white clouds, lander is a small gold box, rocks are low-poly blobs, plinth texture is a dot grid.
Rebuild against `PIA00614` / `PIA00632` (`public/media/thennow/SM-M-002_then_PIA00614-1600.avif`, `…_then-alt_PIA00632-1600.jpg`) and `REF_MARS.md`:
- **Lander opened flat:** base petal + **three triangular side petals lying flat on the ground**, petal tops covered in **dark blue-black solar cells** with thin silver grid lines; whole spread **≈ 2.75 m across**.
- **Deflated airbags:** crumpled **off-white/tan fabric pulled in low against the petal edges** — wrinkled, matte, partly hidden under the petals; they must read as deflated cloth, not inflated spheres.
- **IMP camera** on its mast (**≈ 1.5 m above ground**): small stereo camera head; low-gain antenna; the **ASI/MET weather mast** with its small windsocks on one petal; **ramps unrolled** from a petal to the ground (the rover drove down one).
- **Sojourner** (65 × 48 × 30 cm): six small cleated wheels on rocker-bogie, flat solar panel on top, whip antenna, APXS touching **Yogi** (keep Sonnet's AG-2c scale/position) — rover tracks in the soil from the ramp to Yogi.
- **Ares Vallis landscape (pilot for AG-9b):** no plinth; a rocky flood plain to the horizon — **many** angular rocks of varied size and tilt (instanced, decent normals, dust on top faces), reddish-brown soil with fine texture, **Twin Peaks** as two soft low hills on the horizon, butterscotch dusty sky, warm low sun, long shadows.
- Materials with PBR (matte fabric for airbags, glassy solar cells, anodised/aluminium for mast and rover).
