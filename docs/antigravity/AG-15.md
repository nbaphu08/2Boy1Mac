## Verified structure (NSSDCA 1967-035A; `REF_MOON.md`)
- **About 3 m tall.** A **tripod/tetrahedral space-frame of thin aluminium tubing** with diagonal braces — this triangular frame is the silhouette; every tube must connect at a joint.
- **Central mast** rising **~1 m above the tripod apex**, carrying at its top a **flat solar panel** (0.855 m²) and a **flat planar high-gain antenna**, both on positioners **attached to the mast** (see the photos: a large flat panel tilted, a second flat panel angled).
- **Three hinged landing legs** at the lower corners of the frame, each with a shock-absorber strut and ending in a **small round crushable footpad** (~0.3 m). NSSDCA says the footpads "extended out 4.3 m from the center"; in the Apollo 12 photos the footpad spread is roughly **1.4× the 3 m height** — match the photos.
- **Two thermal compartments** (white boxes with louvres) mounted on the frame; **two small conical omni antennas on folding booms**; small spherical vernier tanks inside the frame. **No retro-rocket** (jettisoned).
- **TV camera mount** with its mirror hood bracket and the **surface-sampler pantograph arm** (1.5 m) — the Apollo 12 crew **took the camera and the scoop home**, so show the mount and arm **without** the camera head and scoop.
- Finish: white paint, bare aluminium tubes, **dust-tan discolouration** (it sat 2.5 years in sunlight); sits on the inner slope of a small crater, LM Intrepid silhouette on the rim (already there — keep).

## Acceptance
Side-by-side with `AS12-48-7133.jpg`, the silhouette matches: triangular frame, mast with two flat panels, three splayed legs with small footpads, no floating or disconnected parts, nothing intersecting the ground. Same camera framing as now (beat 9), 0 console errors.
