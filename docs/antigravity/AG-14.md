## Verified spacecraft facts (eoPortal GRAIL page; NASA/JPL press kit; `REF_MOON.md`)
- Box-shaped composite bus, ~**1.09 m high × 0.95 m wide × 0.76 m deep** ("washing-machine size"), **202 kg dry / 307 kg fuelled**; bus heritage Lockheed Martin XSS-11.
- **Two fixed (non-articulated) solar-array wings**, one on each side of the bus, dark blue-black cells — in the concepts they extend **above and below** the bus in a line (see PIA12009).
- Bus wrapped in **gold multi-layer insulation** with **white radiator panels**; on the face that looks at the twin: the **Ka-band ranging antenna (horn)** — the gold circular feature in the concepts. The ranging antennas sat on the line between the two spacecraft centres; the twins flew **one behind the other, 175–225 km apart**, in a near-polar, near-circular orbit.
- One **22 N main engine** on the −X face, eight small 0.8 N thrusters; S-band patch antennas on ±X; **MoonKAM** camera heads (student imaging program led by Sally Ride Science).
- Do not render any text, logos or numbers.

## 2. Simulation (beat 12 — keep the AG-10 timing and autoplay + `replayGrailSim()` API)
1. **Mapping (0–6 s):** camera behind and slightly above Flow, looking along the orbit at Ebb ahead, the Moon's curve below (like PIA12009). A **thin pulsing blue link** between their Ka horns (signal travelling both ways). As they fly, the **gravity map paints the globe swath by swath** under their ground track; mascons glow red. Then pull back to show the whole painted globe for ~1 s.
2. **Descent (6–11 s):** follow the pair down toward the north-polar mountain near Goldschmidt crater; Ebb ahead, Flow behind.
3. **Impacts (11–13 s):** Ebb strikes first, then Flow, a moment later and about 2 km away: brief additive flash + ballistic ejecta (no air).
4. **Rest:** the mountain slope with **two small craters with dark ejecta**, the twins gone. Ghost holograms are optional and must cast no shadows.
- The model must also be visible clearly in the rest of the beat's framing (no tiny dots): at least ~10 % of frame height during the mapping close-up.
